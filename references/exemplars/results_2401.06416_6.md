For the first surprisal test, our hypothesis is that the mean surprisal of the marker tokens across test examples will be smaller for the control language than for the impossible languages. For the second test, our hypothesis is that the mean surprisal difference across all test pairs will be larger for possible languages than for impossible ones.

Our results are presented in REF. The \mathrm{NoHop} model, which has the verb marking pattern most similar to English, consistently has the lowest mean marker surprisal across training steps in test 1 (REF). The \mathrm{NoHop} model also has the highest mean surprisal difference across training steps in test 2 (REF). Both of these results indicate that GPT-2 has learned to expect the marker tokens when they follow a more natural grammatical pattern and was very surprised when they did not appear at the correct positions.
