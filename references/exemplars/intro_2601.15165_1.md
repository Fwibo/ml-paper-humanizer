Recent research has witnessed a surge in Diffusion Large Language Models (dLLMs)~[cite], which challenge the dominant autoregressive (AR) paradigm~[cite] by treating sequence generation as a discrete denoising process. Central to the appeal of dLLMs is their theoretical flexibility, which offers two distinct advantages over the strict left-to-right causal chain of AR models: efficient parallel decoding and the capability for arbitrary-order generation.

While the efficiency gains of parallel decoding have been well-established~[cite], the implications of arbitrary-order generation remain relatively less explored. Theoretically, the unconstrained generation order constitutes a superset of the fixed autoregressive trajectory and has the potential to unlock more advanced problem-solving abilities. Meanwhile, some works~[cite] have demonstrated the superiority of non-sequential generation for specific tasks such as sudoku and zebra puzzles. Driven by these successes and the theoretical promise, the community has increasingly adopted Reinforcement Learning (RL) to elicit similar advanced capabilities for \emph{general reasoning tasks} such as mathematics and coding~[cite].
