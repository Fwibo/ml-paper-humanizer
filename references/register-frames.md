# Frames of published ML papers, by sentence function

Counted over 307 award and oral papers (2023–2026, NeurIPS / ICML / ICLR / ACL / EMNLP / NAACL / COLM / CVPR / ICCV / ECCV / AAAI / KDD): 1234 frames, each found in at least 6 of those papers after a model fixed the boundary of a statistical candidate and a script re-counted it. A frame is a stock phrase that carries a sentence function; slots are ⟨NP⟩ ⟨clause⟩ ⟨verb⟩ ⟨ref⟩ ⟨N⟩ ⟨X⟩. The `papers` column is how many papers use it. A ★ marks frames that published passages use and model drafts of the same passages almost never do (used in ≥ 5 of 60 original passages, at most a third as often in 318 drafts); these are the field's ordinary connectives and pointers, not rare words.

How to use. Read in full in the frames pass (SKILL.md step 6), after the draft is written and read back: go through the draft sentence by sentence and ask how the authors of these papers would have written it in its place, and revise where the answer differs, with the meaning of the sentence unchanged. Reading this file before writing was tested and changed nothing. The relation connectives (cause, consequence, contrast, concession) are not in this file: they are in [connectives.md](connectives.md), read before writing, and the pass does not touch them.
