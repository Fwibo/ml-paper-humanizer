We evaluate a suite of formal languages grouped by definability in temporal-logic fragments, selecting two representative languages from each class: \begin{itemize} \item \textbf{$\ytl$-definable:} ${{{{StringColor \Sigma}}^*}}{StringColor \texttt{a}}$ (strings ending with ${StringColor \texttt{a}}$) and ${{{{StringColor \Sigma}}^*}}{StringColor \texttt{a}}{StringColor \texttt{b}}$ (strings ending with ${StringColor \texttt{a}}{StringColor \texttt{b}}$).

We consider three attention settings, corresponding to the masking patterns defined in REF: \emph{local-only} (all heads use $\localmask$), \emph{hybrid} (half the heads use $\localmask$, the rest use $\globalmask$), and \emph{global-only} (all heads use $\globalmask$). For local-only and hybrid models, we vary the window size $k \in \{1,2,4\}$. We also evaluate transformers with two positional encodings: sinusoidal encodings ({\texttt{SiPE}}; \citealt{vaswani2017}) and rotary encodings ({\texttt{RoPE}}; \citealt{SU2024127063}). The models are trained on strings of length up to $40$ and evaluated on lengths $41$--$500$. Each experiment is run with $5$ random seeds and $3$ learning rates; additional details are given in REF. In addition to accuracy, we report the \emph{longest perfect length}, defined as the largest length up to which the model achieves $100\%$ accuracy.
