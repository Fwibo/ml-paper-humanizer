# Version history

Not read while writing. Each line says what a version changed and, when something was withdrawn, the reading or measurement that withdrew it. In the tests below a model wrote a draft from the same content as a passage of a published paper, and the draft was compared with the original passage by human reading and by counts.

- v1–v5: word lists and sentence architecture (v1–v2); a claim/reason two-step and authorial register (v3); required devices (v4), withdrawn because the scaffolding read as a template; removals only (v5).
- v6: exemplar passages from award papers.
- v7 / v7.1: a readability layer; withdrawn because it chopped sentences.
- v8: removal rules plus the sentence bank; withdrawn after a reading test.
- v9: v6 plus a dosage note and the bank as read-back material.
- v10 (2026-10-09): every count, position and ban replaced by a reader's need, an anomaly check or an example. Calibration against award papers had shown that a rule written as a count, a position or a ban is applied by a model at every opportunity and becomes a mannerism within one round.
- v10.1 (2026-10-10): v10 drafts were shorter in words, plainer in verbs and more narrated than the originals. The read-back fix was made density-conserving and an exemplar passage became the side-by-side anchor.
- v10.2–v10.5 (2026-10-10/11): a register layer (a cross-field table of verbs and nominals, then a sub-field pass built from neighbouring papers). A blind read of three units ranked the originals first and v10.1 above v10.4/v10.5 every time; the flagged sentences were writer-invented bridges and verb swaps (write → generate, stays ahead → outperforms). The register layer was withdrawn; the section frames added to the playbook in v10.5 were kept.
- v10.6 (2026-10-12): a term ledger (the author's names for things: statistical candidates plus a model's listing, kept only where verified verbatim) and the frames file (stock phrases by sentence function, counted over 307 award and oral papers; ★ marks the frames that original passages use and drafts of the same passages rarely do). Phrase-level mining on 60 originals against 318 drafts showed that what readers called "not senior" and "too professional, tiring" is one gap: the field's ordinary connective and pointer frames, not rarer words.
- v10.7 (2026-10-13): the frames file moves from a read before writing to a pass after the read-back. Read before writing, full or trimmed, it left the share of sentences opening on a frame at 14–19% (originals 65–66%); a light pass raised it to 22–33%, and a thorough sentence-by-sentence pass also brought the field-verb rate close to the originals' (13.4 and 6.7 per 1k words against 13.8 and 10.0). In a labelled reading the light pass was preferred to none and the thorough pass to the light one.
- v10.7.1–v10.7.4 (2026-10-13): the pass narrowed to marking frames (pointer, observation, reading, comparison, scope, sequence, the authors' action, purpose, attention and restatement), asked sentence by sentence; cause and contrast frames added to background facts set side by side had made introductions read as over-linked.
- v10.7.5–v10.7.6 (2026-10-13): where the point sits becomes a read-back question in the terms of Williams and of Gopen and Swan (the point in the main clause, its context in a short clause before it, the subject next to its verb, what the sentence adds at its end). New reader's needs from the general writing guides: the link most published sentences make with no connective, a paragraph's topic string, actions as verbs with their agent as subject, parallel shape for parallel items.
- v10.7.7 (2026-10-13): claim-strength.md gets the reporting verbs by what the evidence is. Measured on 22 originals against 134 drafts, none of five conventions from the writing guides (tense drift, one-sided comparisons, points vs percent, integral citations, hedges) differed beyond noise; reporting verbs did, 68 against 44 per 10k words.
- v10.7.8 (2026-10-13): check_tex flags a dangling comparison (a gap, gain or margin whose paragraph names no sides, table, figure or number); claim-strength.md keeps design intent ("is designed to") in its own clause, apart from outcome.
- v10.7.9 (2026-10-13): connectives leave the pass. Every connective change a post-hoc pass made re-cast a relation the draft already stated (so → Since, yet → Although, because → due to) or guessed a cause from adjacency, and none added information. A relation is now decided when its sentence is written, from connectives.md read once before writing; the pass only reports connectives the reader would not miss. A "no frame in two consecutive sentences" rule was removed because it produced synonym swaps (Since → Given that). The guides section of connectives.md was checked against Strunk 1918, Fowler 1906, Gopen and Swan 1990 and Feak and Swales 1994.
- v10.7.10 (2026-10-13): "one relation per sentence" becomes "one point per sentence"; the stricter rule had chopped an introduction into short sentences (16% under 12 words, against 6% in the original). Statements in positive form; a mechanism's verb is part of its name; an inference carries its reporting verb and is not joined to its premise by "and".
- v10.7.11 (2026-10-13): verbs go the way of connectives, decided when the sentence is written and never upgraded afterwards; the pass had swapped in formal synonyms (exploit, formulate, adopt). Orienting words ride in the opening clause of the sentence they orient, not in a sentence of their own (an introduction had 21% of sentences under 12 words against 6% in the original, nearly all orienting sentences).
- v10.7.12 (2026-10-13): captions say what the table or figure shows and stop. A proposed "colons and semicolons are sparing" rule was withdrawn: the exemplar passages and the drafts had the same rate, about 2.4 colons and 0.9 semicolons per 1,000 words of body prose. check_tex reports a section with three or more, with that rate for reference.
- v10.7.13 (2026-10-16): the read of the frames file's method and result groups before writing is removed (tested with no effect on the frame rate, and the first version with it had the lowest field-verb rate of the series). check_tex stops flagging the result and reading frames the pass is told to add, name-drift? needs a recurring variant that starts with a content word, and "significant" is not flagged when a confidence interval, p-value or test is named nearby. Tested and not adopted: cutting register-frames.md to its ★ rows or to its most frequent rows (readers ranked the full file first, and the cuts lacked frames the pass wanted, such as As shown in, We observe that and In other words, none of them ★ rows); a single-path rewrite mode (reverse outline, fact table, write from the point), where a version revised over several rounds of the author's own reading was preferred to both it and the current mode.
- v10.7.14 (2026-10-16): seven steps and two modes, `rewrite` and `write`; the `review` and `check` modes and two reference files no step read are removed, their live lines moved into SKILL.md. The read-back no longer repeats the frames pass's question. register-frames.md drops an unchecked claim that most ★ frames sit on generic anti-AI ban lists; checked against two humanizer skills, only Furthermore, Moreover, Further, Note that and "the key" are on them.
- v10.7.15 (2026-10-17): release cleanup. The exemplar pool keeps only papers under CC BY 4.0 or CC0 1.0: 144 passages from 30 papers, after 67 passages from 15 papers under the arXiv non-exclusive license, CC BY-NC-ND or CC BY-NC-SA were removed. sentence-bank.md gets a source table with each paper's license. section-playbook.md loses its internal notes and the abstract line "Use two or three numbers at most. Do not write extensive experiments, state-of-the-art, novel or significantly": the one-number line above it and the read-back's hype-word item cover its intent, and claim-strength.md allows "significantly" with a test behind it.
- v10.7.15, same day: renamed from anti-ai-paper-writing to ml-paper-writing-humanizer (name field and title only) and README.md added for the public release. "Anti-AI" reads in English as opposing AI and in Chinese as a detector-bypass tool, and the skill writes as much as it removes. Nothing else on the writing path changed.
- v10.7.15, 2026-10-18: renamed again to ml-paper-humanizer. "Writing humanizer" read as humanizing the act of writing; the skill works on the paper's text. Name field, title, README and install path only.
- v10.7.15, 2026-10-19: README split into README.md (English) and README.zh-CN.md (Chinese), each opening on a before/after example; examples/results-2504.13837.md added with one results section written without the skill, with it, and by the authors. Nothing on the writing path changed.
