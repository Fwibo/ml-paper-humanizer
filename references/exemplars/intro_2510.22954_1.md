Large language models (LMs) are the core backbone of modern AI systems but often fail to produce the diverse, human-like creativity expected in open-ended tasks that do not have a ground truth answer [cite]. This shortfall has sparked growing concerns about the long-term homogenization of human thought, as users are repeatedly exposed to similar outputs [cite]. While ensemble methods or model ``swarms'' have been proposed to enhance diversity [cite], their scalable evaluations of diversity in real-world settings are still lacking [cite]. Existing benchmarks often target stylized tasks such as persona generation [cite], keyword-driven storytelling [cite], or random number generation [cite], and often rely on narrowly defined tests centered on poetry or figurative language [cite]. Yet, these settings fail to capture the open-endedness and pluralism of real-world user interactions.

We introduce \textbf{\mathrm{Infinity-Chat}}, a large-scale dataset of 26K real-world open-ended queries spanning diverse, naturally occurring prompts mined from WildChat~[cite]. These queries admit a wide range of plausible answers with no single correct response. We further develop the first comprehensive taxonomy of open-ended LM queries, encompassing 6 top-level categories (e.g., Brainstorm \& Ideation, and less explored types such as Speculative \& Hypothetical Scenarios, and Skill Development) and 17 subcategories grounded in natural chatbot-user interactions.
