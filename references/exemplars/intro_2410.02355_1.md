Large language models (LLMs) have demonstrated the capability to store extensive knowledge during pre-training and recall it during inference [cite]. Despite this, they frequently exhibit {hallucinations}, producing incorrect or outdated information [cite]. While fine-tuning with updated knowledge offers a straightforward solution, it is often prohibitively time-consuming [cite]. In sight of this, model editing methods have emerged, enabling updating the target knowledge while preserving other knowledge [cite]. Broadly, model editing approaches fall into two categories: (1) {parameter-modifying methods}, which directly adjust a small subset of parameters [cite], and (2) {parameter-preserving methods} that integrate additional modules without altering the original parameters [cite].

In this paper, we aim to explore the parameter-modifying methods for model editing. Concretely, current parameter-modifying methods typically follow the {locate-then-edit} paradigm [cite]. The basic idea is to first locate influential parameters ${\bm{W}}$ through causal tracing, and then edit them by introducing a perturbation $\bm{\Delta}$ [cite].

The common objective for solving $\bm{\Delta}$ is to minimize the output error on the to-be-updated knowledge, denoted as $e_1$. Additionally, the output error on the to-be-preserved knowledge, $e_0$, is typically incorporated into the objective function, acting as a constraint to ensure the model’s accuracy on the preserved knowledge.
