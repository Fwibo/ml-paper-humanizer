In this section, we evaluate In-Run Data Shapley in terms of its efficiency (Section REF), fidelity (Section REF), and its applications in data attribution for language model pretraining (Section REF). In Appendix REF, we compare a variety of existing data attribution methods in small-scale settings.

We compare this to the direct implementation of In-Run Data Shapley, which requires computing per-sample gradients, as well as to regular training without Data Shapley computations. The experiment is conducted by training GPT2-Small on a single 80GB A100 GPU. As illustrated in Table REF, the runtime of first-order In-Run Data Shapley is close to that of regular training when using the ghost dot-product algorithms developed in Section REF. The second-order In-Run Data Shapley is approximately $2\times$ slower than regular training due to the additional backpropagation. However, both the first- and second-order In-Run Data Shapley are significantly faster ($>30\times$) compared to the naive implementation. These results showcase the substantial improvements achieved by our techniques, making In-Run Data Shapley computationally feasible for practical applications.
