Many tasks in scientific and engineering disciplines can be approached through controlled generation of strings from a language model subject to hard constraints. For example, we may seek to generate an API call that matches an endpoint, produce SQL queries that are consistent with the schema of a database, or design a molecule that satisfies a target specification. The dominant approach in these settings is \textbf{locally constrained decoding} (\textbf{LCD}), which forces each sampled token to conform to the constraint [cite].

This approach suffers from two critical drawbacks. First, the typical method for sampling from this distribution by enumerative \textbf{token masking} can be \textit{slow}. Full masking requires checking the constraint against every item in the vocabulary---which can often consist of more than $100,000$ tokens---before finally filtering, renormalizing, and sampling. In some special cases, such as when the constraint can be expressed as a regular or context-free grammar, optimizations are available that make checking every token feasible [cite]. However, for \textbf{black-box constraints}, such optimizations do not apply. Second, as often observed [cite], by renormalizing only the \textit{local} distribution (over tokens), this approach can distort the \textit{global} distribution (over strings). Because LCD myopically enforces constraints at each step, it can sometimes greedily sample its way into low-probability regions of sequence space (see REF for discussion).
