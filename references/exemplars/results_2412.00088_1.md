We applied STDE to amortize the training of PINNs on a set of real-world PDEs. For the case of $k=2$ and large $d$, we tested two types of PDEs: inseparable and effectively high-dimensional PDEs (Appendix REF) and semilinear parabolic PDEs (Appendix REF). We also tested high-order PDEs (Appendix REF) that cover the case of $k=3,4$, which includes PDEs describing 1D and 2D nonlinear dynamics, and high-dimensional PDE with gradient regularization [cite]. Furthermore, we tested a weight-sharing technique (Appendix REF), which further reduces memory requirements (Appendix REF). In all our experiments, STDE drastically reduces computation and memory costs in training PINNs, compared to the baseline method of SDGD with stacked backward-mode AD. Due to the page limit, the most important results are reported here, and the full details including the experiment setup and hyperparameters (Appendix REF) can be found in the Appendix.

PINN [cite] is a class of neural PDE solver where the ansatz \(u_{\theta }(\vb{x})\) is parameterized by a neural network with parameter \(\theta\).
