{blackThe reason the {\tt Token}-specific rule fares better than {\tt OPT} and {\tt Diff} is because the latter compute their deferral decisions based on which of $q_t(\cdot)$ and $p_t(\cdot)$ is more \textit{peaked}; this can be a disadvantage when the sampled token is not close to the distribution mode, which is likely to happen with higher temperatures. As shown in \SREF, with lower temperatures, the gap between these rules diminishes. }

{black \textbf{Few-shot Gemma cascades.} To evaluate the Gemma model cascades, we use few-shot prompting with 8 language benchmarks: WMT, CNN/DM, GSM8K, MBPP, SQuAD 2.0, WebQuestions, NaturalQA and TriviaQA; many of these feature in the SpecBench suite [cite]. Figure REF presents plots of quality vs.\ rejection rate with a 2B drafter and 27B verifier for $\gamma=1$. For brevity, we only compare the methods that fare the best in the previous experiments. With the exception of TriviaQA, {\tt SpecCascade [Token]} is able to both match the 27B's quality at a lower rejection rate and yield the best overall quality, often better than 27B.
